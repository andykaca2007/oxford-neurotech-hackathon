The problem: overload is invisible until something goes wrong

The people whose mistakes cost most are the ones with no gauge
A surgeon at hour nine. A pilot on approach. A controller with twelve aircraft.
They all overload quietly. The mistake comes after, and nothing warned them.
1The brain shows it first.Within seconds of hard thinking, alpha at the back of the head falls and theta at the front rises.
2But no wearable reads it.Lab EEG needs gel and a technician. Dry consumer headsets give 6,000 µV of noise around 30 µV of brain.

The solution: clean the signal hard, score it only when it is clean

A consumer headset, a six-stage cleaning chain, a model that has seen 36 brains, and a number that is zero at your own rest
1Eight dry electrodes, once a second.No gel, no technician; a headset you put on yourself.
2Six filters strip the noise.6,000 µV of drift and hum down to 34 µV of EEG, verified against the live code.
3A model trained on 36 people.Rest vs arithmetic; AUROC 0.74 on people it never saw.
4Your own zero.The number is relative to your rest, with an alarm at 50. Bad signal is reported, never scored.
